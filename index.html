PAUL

KYOTERA POET • READER IN THE FIELD

I am Paul, 20 years, Kyotera Uganda. I write about love in small things - coffee, shirts, keys, ugly rice. Real Ugandan love. Found reading in the fields.

My Poems 🔥 10

THE COFFEE WE SHARE

I wake before the kettle sings your name, The kitchen cold without your sleepy light, I measure two spoons just the same, One strong for you, one soft and light. You shuffle in, your hair a tangled crown, Your hoodie falling off one sleepy shoulder, You steal my mug and never put it down, And suddenly the day is warmer than the coffee.

HOW YOU FOLD MY SHIRTS

You fold my shirts like you mean it, Not perfect, but with care in each crease, Sleeves tucked in, buttons hiding, As if you are telling them peace, Stay here, he will be back.

YOUR KEYS ON MY TABLE

Your keys land loud on my table, A jingle that means you are home, No knock, no text, no fable, Just metal music, you have roamed, Back to where you belong.

GROCERY LIST LOVE

We write our grocery list on the back of bills, Your handwriting big, mine cramped and small, Milk, bread, eggs, and your little frills, Chocolate that you say is for us all, But I know you buy it for me.

WE COOK UGLY RICE

We cook rice that always sticks a bit, You say it is my heat, I say it is your pot, We both are right, we both admit, That perfect rice we have not, But perfect evenings we have.

THE DISHES AT MIDNIGHT

We do dishes at midnight again, Because we procrastinated all day, Plates stacked like our maybe and when, Soaked in water turning grey, Waiting for us to face them.

NIGHT BUS YOUR HEAD ON MY SHOULDER

The night bus smells of diesel and day-old sweat, We take it because taxi is too dear, Your head finds my shoulder, heavy, yet, Light as permission to be near, In public, unashamed.

YOUR UMBRELLA IS TOO SMALL

Your umbrella is too small for two, We walk like crabs, shoulder to shoulder, You tilt it to my side, I tilt to you, We both get wet, just colder, But never let go to stay dry.

HOW YOU STEAL MY HOODIE

You steal my hoodie every cold morning, Not asking, just wearing it like it is yours, You say it smells better in your adorning, Than in my closet behind closed doors, And you are right, it does.

THE WAY YOU CHARGE MY PHONE

My phone dies always at eleven percent, You roll your eyes and plug it in, You say I live too loud, too spent, And never let the low light win, You charge my chaos with your calm.
